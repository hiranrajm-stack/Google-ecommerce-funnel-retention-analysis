
# Business Memo: Google E-Commerce Funnel & Retention Findings

**To:** VP of Product
**From:** Analytics
**Re:** Purchase funnel and retention performance

## Headline
Only 1.5% of users who view a product complete a purchase, and of the
small group who do buy, almost none return — just 4% are still active
one week after their first visit. These are two separate problems with
two separate owners.

## Finding 1: Demand drop-off (Merchandising)
85% of users who view a product never add it to cart. This happens
before any purchase intent is shown — it points to a product, pricing,
or presentation issue, not a technical one. Owner: merchandising/product team.

## Finding 2: Checkout friction (Product/Engineering)
Of users who begin checkout, 85% never complete the purchase. Unlike
Finding 1, these users had already decided to buy. This is a process
problem — extra steps, forced account creation, unexpected costs, or
payment failures are the likely causes. Owner: checkout/payments team.

## Finding 3: Retention gap
Across all 8 cohorts analyzed (Nov 2020–Jan 2021), only ~4% of new
users return the week after their first visit — a consistent pattern,
not a one-off dip. The business currently has no visible mechanism
bringing users back.

## Recommendations
1. Audit the product page experience (pricing clarity, images, reviews)
   to address the view-to-cart drop.
2. Run a checkout funnel audit — session recordings or a simplified
   checkout test — to find the specific friction point.
3. Introduce a basic re-engagement flow (email or retargeting) for
   first-time visitors, since none currently exists.
